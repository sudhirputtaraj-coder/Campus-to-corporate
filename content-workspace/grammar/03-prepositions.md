CAMPUS TO CORPORATE  |  COMMUNICATION SKILLS  |  ENGLISH GRAMMAR

Prepositions: Small Words, Big Impact

Prepositions are the short words that sit in front of a noun or pronoun and tell us how it connects to the rest of the sentence: when something happens, where it is, or where it is going. In the sentence "Please submit the report by Friday", the little word by turns a vague request into a deadline.

They are also one of the biggest sources of mistakes in professional English, because many choices cannot be reasoned out. We say on Monday but in March, interested in but keen on. This module gives you the logic where logic exists, and a clear way to memorise the rest.

## Three questions to choose the right preposition

- Is this about time, place or direction? Time words (at, on, in, for, since) behave differently from place words (at, in, on, above, between).

- Is the preposition tied to a particular word? Many verbs, adjectives and nouns come with a fixed partner (responsible for, depend on, interested in). Learn these as pairs.

- Does this verb need a preposition at all? Some verbs, such as discuss, reach and attend, take none.

## By the end of this module, you will be able to

- Use at, on and in correctly for time and place.

- Choose between for, since, during, by, until and within when talking about time.

- Describe positions and movement with words like above, between, into, through and across.

- Use common verb, adjective and noun partners such as responsible for and interested in.

- Use fixed workplace phrases such as on behalf of, in charge of and due to.

- Avoid unnecessary prepositions, as in "discuss about" or "reach to".

# Part 1: Prepositions of Time: At, On, In and Friends

Time prepositions answer when and how long. The first three, at, on and in, follow a simple pattern: from small and exact to big and general.

| Preposition | Used with | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| at | exact clock times and a few set phrases | at 9:30, at noon, at midnight, at night, at the moment |
| on | days and dates, including parts of a particular day | on Tuesday, on 15 August, on Monday morning, on my birthday |
| in | longer periods: parts of the day, months, seasons, years, centuries | in the evening, in July, in winter, in 2024 |

## Other useful time words

- For tells how long: for two hours, for six months.

- Since tells when something started: since 2021, since Monday. It goes with perfect tenses (I have lived here since 2021).

- During is followed by a noun and means "within that period": during the lecture, during the break. While is followed by a clause: while the lecture was going on.

- By means "no later than" (a deadline): submit by 5 p.m. Until means "continuing up to": wait until 5 p.m.

- Within means "inside a period": reply within two days.

- Before and after place events relative to another event. From...to and between...and mark a range: from 9 to 5, between 2 and 4.

- In can also mean "after this much time from now": The session starts in ten minutes.

Tip: On time means punctual, exactly when planned. In time means early enough, with a little to spare: We reached in time to catch the last train.

# Part 2: Prepositions of Place and Position

Place prepositions answer where. Again, at, in and on are the core three, and the choice depends on how you see the place.

| Preposition | How you see the place | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| at | a point or an address on a map | at the gate, at the bus stop, at the reception, at a conference |
| in | something enclosed or an area you are inside | in the lab, in a lift, in Mysuru, in India, in a car |
| on | a surface, a line, or a floor level | on the table, on the third floor, on the left, on the train |

## Relative position

- Above and below describe higher and lower positions, with no contact needed: the clock above the door, the figures below the graph.

- Over and under often suggest covering or a path directly across: a jacket over a shirt, a bridge over the river, a mat under the chair.

- In front of, behind, next to, beside, opposite and near describe neighbours.

- Between is used for two (or clearly separate) things: between the bank and the temple. Among is used for a group: a speaker among the audience.

- Inside and outside describe containment: waiting outside the office.

Tip: You can be on a bus, train, plane or ship (large transport you can stand in) but in a car, taxi or auto (small, enclosed).

# Part 3: Prepositions of Direction and Movement

When something moves, we describe the path or destination with direction prepositions. Choose the one that matches the movement you are picturing.

| Preposition | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| to | towards a destination | She went to the airport. |
| into | from outside to inside | He walked into the interview room. |
| out of | from inside to outside | She came out of the lift. |
| onto / off | to / from a surface | The cat jumped onto the table and then off it. |
| through | from one end to the other inside something | The train passed through a tunnel. |
| across | from one side to the other of a surface | We walked across the bridge. |
| along | following a line | We strolled along the river. |
| past | going by without stopping | She drove past the college. |
| towards | in the direction of | He ran towards the exit. |
| up / down | to a higher / lower point | They climbed up the hill. |
| from | starting point | I came from the hostel. |

## Home is special

We say go home, come home, reach home with no to. We say go to school, go to work, go to bed with no article. And enter takes no preposition at all: She entered the hall.

Tip: Towards means moving in the direction of something; to means arriving at it. You walk towards the gate, then you get to the gate.

# Part 4: Words That Come with Fixed Prepositions

This is the area where learners lose the most marks, because the preposition is decided by the word before it, not by logic. The best approach is to learn them in pairs, in small groups, and to read them in full sentences.

## Adjective + preposition

| Pair | Example |
| --- | --- |
| good at / bad at | She is good at data analysis. |
| interested in | He is interested in cybersecurity. |
| afraid of / proud of / aware of | We are proud of our team. |
| responsible for | Who is responsible for the budget? |
| different from | This plan is different from the last one. |
| keen on / fond of | She is keen on joining the startup. |
| capable of | You are capable of leading a team. |

## Verb + preposition

| Pair | Example |
| --- | --- |
| depend on / rely on | Our success depends on teamwork. |
| apply for (a job) / apply to (a company) | I applied for the role at Wipro. |
| wait for | Please wait for my signal. |
| listen to | Listen to the instructions carefully. |
| belong to | This laptop belongs to the department. |
| believe in / succeed in | She succeeded in clearing the aptitude round. |
| apologise for (a mistake) / to (a person) | He apologised to the client for the delay. |
| congratulate on | We congratulated her on her promotion. |
| participate in / contribute to | Everyone contributed to the project. |
| look forward to + -ing | I look forward to meeting you. |

## Noun + preposition

A reason for the delay, a solution to the problem, an increase in sales, a demand for skilled workers, a lack of experience, a reply to your email.

Tip: After a preposition, a verb takes the -ing form: interested in learning, good at writing, look forward to hearing. This is why "I look forward to meet you" is wrong.

# Part 5: Workplace Phrases and Fixed Expressions

Some prepositions appear in phrases you will meet in every office, email and interview. Treat them as ready-made blocks and learn them whole.

| Phrase | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| on behalf of | representing someone | I am writing on behalf of the placement cell. |
| in charge of | responsible for managing | Rekha is in charge of recruitment. |
| in addition to | as well as | In addition to Java, we need SQL. |
| because of / due to | caused by (followed by a noun) | The session was cancelled due to rain. |
| instead of | in place of | Let us meet on Friday instead of Thursday. |
| in spite of / despite | even though (followed by a noun) | Despite the delay, we met the deadline. |
| according to | as stated by | According to the schedule, the test is at 11. |
| with regard to | about | With regard to your query, please see below. |
| at the end / in the end | at the final point / finally | At the end of the day, we won. In the end, she accepted. |
| by hand / by email | the method or means | Please send the form by email. |
| in touch with | in contact with | I will stay in touch with the recruiter. |

Tip: Because of, due to, in spite of and despite are followed by a noun or -ing form, not by a full clause. Say "because of the rain" but "because it rained".

# Part 6: No Preposition Needed: Spot and Fix

Adding a preposition that does not belong is just as common as choosing the wrong one. These verbs already contain the preposition's meaning, so they take their object directly.

| Verb | Wrong | Right |
| --- | --- | --- |
| discuss | We discussed about the plan. | We discussed the plan. |
| reach | She reached to the office. | She reached the office. |
| attend | He attended to the meeting. | He attended the meeting. |
| enter | They entered into the hall. | They entered the hall. |
| mention | She mentioned about it. | She mentioned it. |
| emphasise | He emphasised on punctuality. | He emphasised punctuality. |
| marry | She married with Rohan. | She married Rohan. |
| resemble | He resembles to his father. | He resembles his father. |
| approach | I approached to the manager. | I approached the manager. |

## Careful: some verbs do need a preposition

Talk about is fine because talk is a different verb: We talked about the plan. Similarly, listen to, wait for, look at, search for and ask for need their prepositions. And note that many learners add the wrong one: it is explain it to me (not explain me), suggest an idea to me (not suggest me), tell me (not tell to me).

Tip: If you are unsure, test with the noun form. We say a discussion about the plan, but the verb discuss takes no about.

# Quick Recap

- At = exact time or point; on = day, date, surface; in = period, area, enclosed space.

- For = how long; since = from when; during + noun; while + clause.

- By = deadline; until = continuing up to; within = inside a period.

- To = destination; into = going inside; through, across, along, past, towards = path words.

- Learn fixed pairs: good at, interested in, responsible for, depend on, apply for.

- After a preposition, a verb takes -ing.

- Discuss, reach, attend, enter, mention and emphasise take no preposition.

- Because of, due to, in spite of and despite take a noun, not a full clause.
