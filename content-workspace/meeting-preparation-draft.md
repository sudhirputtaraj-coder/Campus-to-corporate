# Authoring draft: Prepare for a meeting and follow through

Status: Draft for review. Not imported or published.
Parent skill: Professionalism. Suggested module: Reliable workplace habits.
Suggested duration: 15 minutes.
Outcome: Prepare a useful update, clarify ownership and send an accurate follow-up.

## Learner text

## Why preparation matters
A meeting is useful when people leave knowing what was decided and what happens next. As a new team member, you can contribute by preparing a short update and recording clear actions.

## Workplace example
You are helping a team prepare a product demonstration. Your slides are ready, but one figure still needs checking. The demonstration is on Friday. Before the planning meeting, write down what is complete, what is uncertain and the decision you need from the team.

## Before the meeting
- Read the agenda and relevant material.
- Prepare a short update: completed work, next step and any blocker.
- Bring a specific request: “Who can verify the customer count by Thursday afternoon?”
- Check access to the meeting link and documents in advance.

## During the meeting
Listen before responding. If an action is unclear, ask: “Who owns this, and when should it be completed?” Repeat important details to confirm your understanding. Do not record agreement that nobody actually gave.

## After the meeting
Send a brief summary containing decisions, action owners and agreed deadlines. Keep open questions separate from decisions. If the deadline becomes unrealistic, notify the owner early and suggest a revised plan.

## Your turn
Spend five minutes drafting a follow-up for this situation:
The team agreed that you will prepare the slide draft by Wednesday at 3 p.m. Ravi will verify the customer count by Thursday at noon. The rehearsal time was discussed but not decided.

## Check your work
Your summary should assign the slide draft and customer-count check to the correct people with the agreed deadlines. List rehearsal timing as an open question. Ask who will confirm it; do not invent an agreed time.

## Takeaway
Prepare briefly, clarify responsibility and follow through on what was actually agreed.

## Self-study practice — add separately

Question: The meeting ends with “someone should update the slides.” What clarification would help?
Answer: Ask who owns the update, what needs changing and when it is due. Confirm those details before recording the action.

Question: You realise your agreed deadline may slip. What should you do?
Answer: Inform the relevant owner early, explain the blocker and propose a realistic next step or revised deadline.
